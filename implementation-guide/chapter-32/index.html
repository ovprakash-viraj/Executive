Go-Live Is Where the Plan Meets Operational Reality

Production deployment is the controlled execution of the cutover plan and the moment the new operating model becomes real.

The objective is not simply to complete technical deployment. It is to start the business safely, validate critical processes quickly and maintain clear decision authority throughout the event.

32.1 Establish the Go-Live Command Structure

Confirm cutover lead, workstream leads, technical leads, business validators, communications owner and executive decision authority.

Use one agreed source of status during the event.

32.2 Execute the Runbook with Evidence

Mark activities complete only when the required evidence exists. Record actual start, finish, issues and decisions.

Avoid skipping steps because the event appears ahead of schedule.

32.3 Control Production Changes

Freeze unrelated changes and allow only authorized deployment content and emergency fixes.

Every production change during go-live should be traceable.

32.4 Validate Critical Business Processes

Run agreed smoke tests and early business transactions across high-risk flows, roles and integrations.

Validation should confirm both transaction success and downstream results.

32.5 Monitor Data and Integration Health

Watch interface queues, batch jobs, reconciliations, error logs and operational dashboards.

Early detection reduces the time between failure and business impact.

32.6 Communicate by Exception and Milestone

Provide concise status at agreed checkpoints and escalate material deviations immediately.

Do not flood leadership with low-value operational detail while hiding the few decisions that matter.

32.7 Manage Go-Live Incidents

Use defined severity, ownership and war-room practices. Separate genuine incidents from user questions and enhancement requests.

Record temporary workarounds and ensure emergency fixes are reconciled into ALM afterward.

32.8 Confirm Business Start and Handover to Hypercare

When critical validation is complete, formally confirm the business-start status and transition open issues into the hypercare operating model.

The end of cutover should have a clear acceptance point.

From the Delivery Floor

During production deployment, an integration queue began building even though transactions appeared successful in the source system. Monitoring identified the issue before users reported it.

The team paused dependent processing, corrected the configuration and replayed the messages using the documented recovery path. Early operational monitoring prevented a wider business interruption.

32.9 The Delivery Leader's View

Go-live leadership depends on calm prioritization. Protect the runbook, make deviations visible and ensure decisions are made by the people with authority rather than by whoever happens to be closest to the issue.

Chapter 32 Implementation Checklist

  • Is the command structure active?
  • Is one source of status used?
  • Are runbook tasks evidence-based?
  • Are unrelated production changes frozen?
  • Are critical smoke tests complete?
  • Are integrations and batches monitored?
  • Are incidents triaged consistently?
  • Are emergency changes traceable?
  • Is business start formally confirmed?
  • Are remaining issues handed to hypercare?

Key Takeaways

Go-live is operational execution, not only technical deployment.

Evidence should drive task completion.

Production change must remain controlled under pressure.

Early monitoring and business validation reduce impact.

Cutover ends only when the business start is formally confirmed.

Closing Thought

The best go-live events are not the ones with no surprises. They are the ones in which surprises are detected early, owned quickly and managed without losing control.
