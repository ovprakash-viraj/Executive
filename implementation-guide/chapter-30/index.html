Cutover Is the Most Compressed Delivery Plan in the Program

Cutover brings together data migration, configuration, integrations, security, business operations, communications and support within a tightly controlled window.

It should be planned and rehearsed as an integrated operational event, not assembled from separate workstream checklists at the last moment.

30.1 Define the Cutover Scope

List every activity required to stop, migrate, configure, validate, communicate and start the new operating model.

Include business tasks such as transaction freezes, manual workarounds and opening activities.

30.2 Build a Dependency-Based Runbook

Sequence tasks with owner, planned start, duration, predecessor, evidence and decision point.

A timestamped checklist is useful only when dependencies and acceptance criteria are understood.

30.3 Identify the Critical Path

Determine which cutover activities control the earliest possible business start. Protect specialist capacity around these tasks.

Model what happens if a critical task slips.

30.4 Plan Freeze and Delta Handling

Define when legacy transactions stop, how late changes are captured and whether delta migration is required.

Communicate freeze rules clearly to operational teams.

30.5 Define Validation and Reconciliation

Specify technical checks, business smoke tests, financial reconciliation and responsible sign-off.

Validation should be fast enough for the cutover window but strong enough to support the go/no-go decision.

30.6 Prepare Rollback and Contingency Options

Define what conditions would trigger rollback or alternative operating procedures and what rollback is technically possible.

Not every deployment can be reversed quickly; know the decision before the event.

30.7 Establish the Command Structure

Define cutover lead, workstream leads, bridge or war-room channels, escalation route and status cadence.

Decision authority must be available when time-sensitive issues occur.

30.8 Rehearse the Full Event

Run cutover simulations using realistic timing, dependencies and data volumes. Capture lessons and update the runbook.

A rehearsal should test coordination as well as technical execution.

From the Delivery Floor

A program had separate migration, deployment and business-readiness plans, each of which looked complete. During rehearsal, teams discovered overlapping freezes and conflicting assumptions about when integrations could be enabled.

An integrated cutover runbook exposed the dependencies and reduced the production window by eliminating unnecessary waiting between teams.

30.9 The Delivery Leader's View

Cutover readiness is achieved when the organization can explain who does what, in what sequence, with what evidence and what happens if the plan deviates. Unowned activities or untested assumptions should be treated as go-live risks.

Chapter 30 Implementation Checklist

  • Is cutover scope complete across technical and business work?
  • Are tasks dependency-linked?
  • Is the critical path understood?
  • Are transaction freezes and deltas defined?
  • Are validation steps explicit?
  • Are rollback conditions understood?
  • Is decision authority available?
  • Is the command structure clear?
  • Has the runbook been rehearsed?
  • Have rehearsal lessons been incorporated?

Key Takeaways

Cutover is an integrated operational event.

Sequence and dependency matter more than checklist volume.

Validation and decision points must be designed into the runbook.

Rollback assumptions should be explicit.

Rehearsal converts cutover from theory into evidence.

Closing Thought

A successful cutover feels controlled because the difficult decisions were made before the clock started.
